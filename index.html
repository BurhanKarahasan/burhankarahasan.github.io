Research

Current · Flagship project

Closed-loop control of small-scale robots under X-ray imaging

  • Closed-loop control
  • X-ray imaging
  • Perception
  • Real-time systems

X-ray fluoroscopy is the imaging actually used in clinical interventions, so a small robot that can be steered autonomously under X-ray is a step toward robots that can be deployed in vivo. I built a closed-loop platform for this inside a cabinet X-ray system, end to end:

  • Hardware: three motorized linear stages, plus a servo and a stepper at the end-effector.
  • Feedback: live images tapped from the X-ray unit, with calibration between image and stage coordinates.
  • Perception: I adapted an existing neural-network detector from prior published work to locate the robot in each frame.
  • Control: a finite-state machine that sends small stepwise position commands. I chose it over PID because the stepper stages are driven over USB with latency, which made continuous feedback unreliable; the state machine gave stable, precise navigation.

Presented as a poster at the 7th Turkish Robotics Conference (July 2026); a first-author journal manuscript extending it is in preparation.
